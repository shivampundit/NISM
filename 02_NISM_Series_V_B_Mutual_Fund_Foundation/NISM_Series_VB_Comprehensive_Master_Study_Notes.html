Chapter 1: Investment Landscape (Weightage: 6% · 3 Questions)

1.1 Financial Goals, Saving vs. Investment & Inflation

  • Saving vs. Investing: Saving is keeping aside money from current income for future needs with emphasis on capital preservation and high liquidity (e.g., savings bank account, post office savings). Investing commits saved capital to productive assets to generate positive real returns over time. Rule: Saving always precedes investing, though both are often used interchangeably in casual usage.
  • Four Broad Financial Needs: (1) Transaction needs (day-to-day living expenses requiring liquid cash), (2) Protection needs (contingencies, medical emergencies, life/general insurance), (3) Investment needs (wealth creation for milestone goals like education, home purchase), and (4) Retirement needs (Accumulation Phase during working years vs. Distribution Phase post-retirement).
  • Future Value & Inflation Formula: Inflation erodes purchasing power. If a goal costs PV today and inflation is i per annum over n years, the future money requirement is FV = PV × (1 + i)n.
Worked Numerical Example (Workbook Sapna Case Study)

Sapna requires Rs. 10,00,000 in today's money terms for her daughter's higher education after 10 years. Expected education inflation is 8% p.a.
Future Requirement = 10,00,000 × (1 + 0.08)10 = 10,00,000 × 2.158925 = Rs. 21,58,925 (≈ Rs. 21.59 Lakh).
Similarly, an expense of Rs. 50,000 today at 10% inflation for 3 years requires 50,000 × (1 + 0.10)3 = Rs. 66,550.

1.2 Goal Classification Matrix & Evaluation Factors

  • Time Horizon Classification: Immediate term (up to 1 year), Near term (1 to 3 years), Medium term (3 to 5 years), and Long term (beyond 5 years).
  • Priority Classification: Critically Important (non-negotiable goals: children's education, retirement, primary residence), Dreams (aspirational upgrades: bigger house, international vacation), and Good-to-have (discretionary lifestyle purchases).
  • Seven Investment Evaluation Parameters: (1) Safety of principal, (2) Liquidity (ease of conversion to cash without value loss), (3) Returns (Regular income vs. Capital appreciation), (4) Convenience (ease of buying, holding, servicing), (5) Ticket Size (minimum investment required), (6) Taxability of Income (post-tax return matters most), and (7) Tax Deduction (e.g., Section 80C under old tax regime).

Chapter 2: Concept and Role of a Mutual Fund (Weightage: 12% · 6 Questions)

2.1 Concept, Key Terms & Role of Mutual Funds

  • Investing "Through" Mutual Funds: Technically, an investor does not invest in a mutual fund; one invests through a mutual fund into underlying asset classes (equities, bonds, gold, real estate). Each scheme must have a clearly stated Investment Objective.
  • Core Accounting Terms:
    • Face Value: Standard face value of a mutual fund unit is Rs. 10.
    • Unit Capital: Total number of outstanding units × Face Value (Rs. 10).
    • Assets Under Management (AUM): Total market value of all portfolio investments plus current assets across a scheme or AMC.
    • Net Asset Value (NAV): Indicates how much money can be generated per unit if the scheme is liquidated: NAV = (Market Value of Investments + Current Assets − Current Liabilities & Accrued Expenses) ÷ Total Outstanding Units.
    • Mark to Market (MTM): Daily valuation of every security in the scheme's portfolio at current market prices so investors enter and exit at fair value.
    • IDCW (Income Distribution cum Capital Withdrawal): Effective April 1, 2021, SEBI renamed the "Dividend Option" to IDCW to clarify that payouts are drawn from the investor's own accumulated scheme surplus and reduce NAV by the exact payout amount.
  • Key Advantages vs. Limitations:
    • Advantages: Professional management, affordable portfolio diversification (even with Rs. 500), economies of scale, high transparency, liquidity, tax deferral (Section 10(23D)), tax deduction under Section 80C (ELSS up to Rs. 1.50 lakh in old regime), convenient systematic options (SIP, SWP, STP), and strict regulatory oversight by SEBI.
    • Limitations: Lack of portfolio customization (a unit holder cannot instruct the fund manager which stock to buy/sell, unlike PMS), choice overload (2,000+ scheme variants), no investor control over scheme operating costs (within SEBI caps), and no guaranteed returns.

2.2 Structural Classification of Mutual Fund Schemes

  • Open-Ended Schemes: No fixed maturity date; variable unit capital; investors can buy and redeem units directly with the fund on any business day at Applicable NAV.
  • Close-Ended Schemes: Have a fixed maturity date; units are issued only during the New Fund Offer (NFO) and redeemed at maturity. To provide interim liquidity, SEBI mandates compulsory listing on a stock exchange (except ELSS). Close-ended units on exchanges typically trade at a discount to NAV due to low secondary market liquidity.
  • Interval Funds: Combine features of open-ended and close-ended schemes. Largely close-ended, but become open-ended during pre-specified Transaction Periods (minimum 2 days) separated by an Interval Period (minimum 15 days) between two transaction periods. Compulsorily listed on stock exchanges.
  • Exchange Traded Funds (ETFs): Passive open-ended funds tracking an index or commodity (Gold ETFs, Silver ETFs) whose units are listed and traded on stock exchanges throughout the day at real-time market prices like equity shares (requires Demat + Trading account).
  • MF Lite Framework (SEBI Circular Dec 2024): Simplified regulatory framework with relaxed net-worth, track-record, and compliance rules specifically designed for AMCs managing exclusively passive schemes (Index Funds, ETFs, and passive FoFs).

Only one scheme per category is permitted per AMC, except for Index Funds/ETFs, Fund of Funds, Sectoral/Thematic Funds, and Target Maturity/Life Cycle Funds.

2.5 Specialized & Emerging Fund Categories & AMFI Growth Milestones

  • Target Maturity Funds (TMFs): Passive open-ended debt index funds or ETFs with a specified maturity date (tenure 3 to 14 years / 2 to 14 years) investing in constituent bonds of a target maturity index.
  • Specialized Investment Fund (SIF — New 2026 Update): Bridges the gap between traditional mutual funds and PMS, offering greater portfolio flexibility (including permitted long-short derivatives strategies). Requires a minimum investment of Rs. 10 Lakhs per investor across all SIF strategies at the PAN level within the same AMC (exception: mandatory investments by AMC designated employees).
  • ESG Investing (6 Permitted Strategies): (1) Exclusion, (2) Integration, (3) Best-in-class & Positive Screening, (4) Impact Investing, (5) Sustainable Objectives, and (6) Transition / Transition-related investments.
  • Smart Beta, Factor & Quant Funds: Factor/Smart Beta funds are rule-based passive/semi-active strategies targeting specific factors (Value, Size, Momentum, Low Volatility, Quality, Dividend Yield). Quant funds use mathematical/statistical models selected by fund managers with limited human intervention.
  • Infrastructure Debt Funds (IDFs): Can be set up either as a Trust (regulated by SEBI) or as a Company/NBFC (regulated by RBI); minimum investment Rs. 1 crore with Rs. 10 lakh minimum unit size.
  • Industry Growth Data (AMFI March 2026 Update): Indian MF industry AUM grew over 6-fold from Rs. 12.33 lakh crore (March 2016) to Rs. 79.46 lakh crore (March 2026). Monthly SIP inflows surged from Rs. 8,055 crore (March 2019) to Rs. 32,087 crore (March 2026).

Chapter 3: Legal Structure of Mutual Funds in India (Weightage: 8% · 4 Questions)

3.1 Three-Tier Trust Structure under SEBI (Mutual Funds) Regulations, 2026

  • Mutual Fund as a Trust: Governed by the SEBI (Mutual Funds) Regulations, 2026 (which replaced the 1996 regulations) and established under the Indian Trusts Act, 1882. The Trust Deed is executed by the Sponsor in favour of the Trustees and registered under the Indian Registration Act, 1908. Unitholders are the beneficiaries of the trust.
  • Sponsor: Promoter who establishes the mutual fund (akin to a company promoter). Eligibility: minimum 5 years of experience in financial services, positive net worth in all immediately preceding 5 years, and contributes at least 40% of the net worth of the AMC (any person holding 40% or more of AMC net worth is deemed a Sponsor).
  • Trustees (Regulation 11 of SEBI MF Regulations, 2026): Hold scheme property in trust for the benefit of unitholders. Can be a Board of Trustees (minimum 4 individuals) or a Trustee Company. At least two-thirds (2/3rds) of the trustees/directors must be independent and not associated with the Sponsor. Prior SEBI approval is mandatory before appointing any trustee.
  • Asset Management Company (AMC): Appointed by Trustees via an Investment Management Agreement (IMA) to handle day-to-day management of the mutual fund. At least 50% of AMC directors must be independent. An AMC of one mutual fund cannot be an AMC or trustee of another mutual fund. Appointment of an AMC can be terminated by a majority of Trustees OR by 75% of the unitholders of the scheme.
  • Unit Holder Protection Committee (UHPC — 2026 Mandate): Under SEBI (Mutual Funds) Regulations, 2026, every AMC is mandated to constitute a Unit Holder Protection Committee (UHPC) responsible for reviewing investor grievances, unclaimed amounts, regulatory compliance, and investor awareness initiatives, reporting directly to the AMC Board and Trustees.
  • Custodian: Registered with SEBI under the SEBI (Custodian) Regulations, 1996; appointed by Trustees to hold and safeguard scheme securities, settle delivery/receipt of trades, and track corporate actions (dividends, bonuses, rights). Independence rule: A custodian in which the Sponsor or its associates hold 50% or more voting rights or directors cannot act as custodian unless stringent net-worth and independent-director conditions are met.

3.2 Other Key Constituents, AMFI & Cyber Security (CSCRF / FIRE 2026 Updates)

  • Registrar and Transfer Agency (RTA): Registered with SEBI; maintains investor records, processes purchases, redemptions, switches, and issues account statements (e.g., CAMS, KFintech). Exam Trap: An AMC can either appoint an independent RTA OR choose to carry out the RTA function in-house (hence the statement "RTA function cannot be retained in-house" is FALSE).
  • Fund Accountant: Calculates daily NAV by accounting for assets, liabilities, and expenses. Unlike Custodians and RTAs, a Fund Accountant does NOT require separate registration with SEBI (can be done in-house or outsourced).
  • Auditors: Scheme Auditor is appointed by the Trustees; AMC Auditor is appointed by the AMC. Both auditors must be different entities to avoid conflict of interest.
  • Valuation Agencies & Credit Rating Agencies: AMFI has appointed two valuation agencies—CRISIL Ltd. and ICRA Ltd.—to provide security-level valuation prices for debt instruments.
  • Association of Mutual Funds in India (AMFI): Incorporated on August 22, 1995 as a non-profit association of all SEBI-registered AMCs. AMFI is NEITHER a regulatory body NOR a Self-Regulatory Organization (SRO); it is an industry association that issues ARN numbers, enforces the AMFI Code of Ethics (ACE) and AGNI, and promotes investor awareness.
  • Cyber Security & Cyber Resilience Framework (CSCRF — May & August 2026 Updates):
    • Built on 5 core cyber resiliency goals from CERT-In's Cyber Crisis Management Plan (CCMP): Anticipate, Withstand, Contain, Recover, and Evolve.
    • SEBI established the AI task force cyber-suraksha.ai (SEBI Circular dated May 5, 2026) to strengthen AI-driven cyber resilience in securities markets.
    • Under SEBI Circular dated August 24, 2026 aligned with the Financial Stability Board's (FSB) Format for Incident Reporting Exchange (FIRE) framework, regulated entities must report cyber incidents to mkt_incidents@sebi.gov.in within 6 hours of detection and submit the detailed report on the SEBI Incident Reporting Portal within 24 hours.

Chapter 4: Mutual Fund Products for the New Cadre of Distributors (Weightage: 6% · 3 Questions)

4.1 Eligibility & Permitted Simple and Performing Schemes

Introduced via SEBI Circular CIR/IMD/DF/21/2012 dated September 13, 2012 to expand mutual fund penetration into semi-urban and rural India, the New Cadre of Distributors obtains an ARN after passing NISM-Series-V-B: Mutual Fund Foundation Certification Examination (with simplified KYD and exemption from biometric verification).

  • Eligible Categories of Individuals:
    1. Postal agents,
    2. Retired government and semi-government officials (Class III and above) with at least 10 years of service,
    3. Retired teachers with at least 10 years of service,
    4. Retired bank officers with at least 10 years of service, and
    5. Bank correspondents appointed by scheduled commercial banks.
  • Permitted "Simple and Performing" Mutual Fund Schemes (5 Allowed Categories):
    1. Liquid Schemes and Money Market Schemes (lowest interest rate and credit risk among debt funds),
    2. Index Schemes (passively tracking a market index with at least 95% allocation; zero fund manager selection risk),
    3. Diversified Equity Schemes (having a track record of returns equal to or better than their scheme benchmark in each of the immediately preceding 3 years),
    4. Retirement Benefit Schemes / Life Cycle Funds (open-ended target date glide-path funds with 5–30 year tenures and graded exit loads of 3%/2%/1%), and
    5. Fixed Maturity Plans (FMPs) and Target Maturity Funds (TMFs) (close-ended debt FMPs with locked-in yields and passive open-ended TMFs with 3 to 14 years maturity).
  • Excluded Schemes for New Cadre: Complex or high-risk schemes such as Credit Risk Funds, Dynamic Bond Funds, Gilt Funds, Arbitrage Funds, Sectoral/Thematic Equity Funds, Focused Funds, and Specialized Investment Funds (SIFs) are NOT permitted to be distributed under NISM-Series-V-B.

Chapter 5: Scheme Related Information (Weightage: 12% · 6 Questions)

5.1 Mandatory Legal Documents: SID, SAI, KIM & Addendum

Mutual fund documents are prepared strictly in the format prescribed by SEBI. SEBI does not "approve" or certify the merits of any scheme; draft documents are "vetted" by SEBI for disclosure compliance. Final documents must be hosted on the AMFI website at least 2 days before the NFO opens.

Document Core Contents & Investor Utility Mandatory Updation Timeline
SID (Scheme Information Document) Scheme-specific details: investment objective, asset allocation, investment strategies, benchmark, risk factors, fund manager, load structure, TER, and fundamental attributes. Draft available on SEBI website for 21 working days. Updated half-yearly within 1 month from the end of September and March.
SAI (Statement of Additional Information) Statutory fund-level details: Sponsors, Trustees, AMC directors, Custodian, RTA, condensed financial information (for schemes launched in last 3 FYs), investor rights, and legal disclosures. Updated within 3 months from the end of every financial year (i.e., by June 30); material changes updated via addendum within 7 days.
KIM (Key Information Memorandum) Concise summary of SID and SAI; mandatorily attached to every application form so investors review key scheme parameters before signing. Updated at least once a half-year within 1 month from the end of September and March.
Addendum Official notice of interim changes (e.g., change of fund manager, branch address, exit load, or TER) which automatically becomes an integral part of SID/SAI/KIM. Issued as and when changes occur; hosted on AMC website and published in newspapers where mandated.

5.2 Fundamental Attributes & Mandatory Disclosures (April 2026 TER Update)

  • Change in Fundamental Attributes (Regulation 18(15A)): Fundamental attributes include type of scheme (open/close-ended), investment objective, asset allocation pattern, and terms of issue (liquidity/fees). To change any fundamental attribute, the AMC must obtain prior Trustee and SEBI approval, publish an advertisement in 1 English national daily and 1 regional newspaper, and give existing unitholders an exit window of not less than 30 days at prevailing NAV without any exit load.
  • Total Expense Ratio (TER) Formula (Effective April 1, 2026): Under the revised SEBI framework effective April 1, 2026, Base Expense Ratio (BER) is separated from brokerage, transaction costs, and statutory levies:

    Total Expense Ratio (TER) = Base Expense Ratio (BER) + Brokerage Cost + Transaction Cost + Statutory Levies (including GST, STT, Stamp Duty)

    AMCs must disclose scheme-wise TER (Direct and Regular plans separately) on a daily basis on the AMC and AMFI websites in Format No. 7E. Any single expenditure item exceeding 10% of total scheme expenses must be disclosed separately in the annual report.
  • Product Labeling & 6-Level Risk-o-meter: Depicts the risk level of both the Scheme AND its Benchmark side-by-side across 6 levels: (1) Low, (2) Low to Moderate, (3) Moderate, (4) Moderately High, (5) High, and (6) Very High. Evaluated monthly and disclosed by the 10th of the succeeding month (along with annual history of risk-o-meter changes by March 31).
  • Mandatory Disclosure Deadlines:
    • Daily NAV: Every business day on AMC & AMFI websites (by 11:00 PM; 10:00 AM next business day for FoFs). Liquid and Overnight Funds must compute and declare NAV for all calendar days including Sundays and non-business holidays. Close-ended schemes declare NAV at least once a week.
    • Portfolio Disclosure: Monthly within 10 days of the close of each month and half-year for all schemes. For Debt Schemes, portfolio disclosure is mandatory on a fortnightly basis within 5 days of every fortnight (15th and month-end), including security-wise yield.
    • Unaudited Financial Results: Within 1 month from the close of each half-year (March 31 and September 30).
    • Scheme Annual Report: Within 4 months from the close of the financial year (i.e., on or before July 31).
    • Non-Mandatory Disclosure: The Monthly Fund Factsheet is a voluntary marketing/informational publication (not a statutory document under SEBI regulations), though once published it must adhere to the SEBI Advertisement Code.

Chapter 6: Fund Distribution and Channel Management Practices (Weightage: 10% · 5 Questions)

6.1 Distribution Channels, Digital Aggregators & ARN Prerequisites

  • Channel Types: Individual Distributors vs. Institutional Distributors (Banks, NBFCs, National/Regional Distributors, and SEBI-registered Execution Only Platforms (EOPs) introduced via June 13, 2023 circular).
  • Stock Exchange & Industry Aggregator Platforms:
    • Stock Exchange Platforms: BSE StAR MF and NSE NMF-II (non-demat as well as demat order routing).
    • MF Utilities (MFU): Industry-shared transaction aggregation platform promoted by AMCs that assigns a single Common Account Number (CAN) to consolidate all mutual fund holdings of an investor and enables multi-AMC investments using a single Common Transaction Form (CTF) and single payment.
    • MFCentral: Unified investor services and transaction portal jointly launched by the two Qualified RTAs—CAMS and KFintech—pursuant to SEBI directive.
  • Mandatory Prerequisites to Become a Distributor: (1) Minimum age of 18 years, (2) Passing NISM-Series-V-A (or NISM-Series-V-B for the New Cadre), (3) Completing KYD (Know Your Distributor) with PAN + address proof (biometric exempted for new cadre), and (4) Obtaining AMFI Registration Number (ARN) (valid for 3 years; renewed via CPE or re-exam; individuals aged 50+ or with 10+ years experience as of May 31, 2010 are eligible for CPE). Corporate distributor sales employees must obtain an Employee Unique Identification Number (EUIN).

6.2 Distributor Revenue, Full Trail Model & 2025–2026 Commission Reforms

Critical September 2026 Regulatory Updates on Commissions
  • Abolition of Upfront Commission & Transaction Charges (SEBI Circular Aug 2025 & Master Circular 20 March 2026):Mutual funds follow a strict 100% Full Trail Commission Model (Regulation 66(6) of SEBI MF Regulations, 2026). Upfront commissions and investor-borne transaction charges (which were earlier Rs. 100 / Rs. 150 on subscriptions ≥ Rs. 10,000) have been completely discontinued.
  • Additional Commission for B-30 & Women Investors (Regulation 52(4A) & SEBI Circular Nov 27, 2025): Paid out of the 2 bps investor education/inclusion accrual for inflows from: (a) New individual investors (new PAN) from B-30 cities, and (b) New women individual investors (new PAN) from BOTH Top-30 and B-30 cities (no dual incentive if a woman investor is from B-30). Incentive rate: 1% of the first lump sum application (or first-year SIP investments), capped at a maximum of Rs. 2,000, subject to a minimum 1-year holding period (clawed back if redeemed within 1 year).
  • Two-Tranche GST Commission Payout (AMFI Best Practices Circular dated 12 March 2026): Since BER excludes statutory levies, AMCs pay trail commission in two tranches: (1) Base commission (exclusive of GST) to all distributors, and (2) GST component separately only to GST-registered distributors (aggregate annual turnover > Rs. 20 Lakhs) upon submission of a valid GST invoice.
  • Daily Trail Commission Formula: Trail commission is calculated daily on the current market value (AUM) of the investor's holdings and paid monthly:

    Daily Trail Commission = (Investor AUM on that Day × Annual Trail Commission Rate) ÷ 365

    For example, on an AUM of Rs. 10,00,000 at a trail rate of 1.00% p.a., daily trail = (10,00,000 × 0.01) ÷ 365 = Rs. 27.40 per day. No commission is payable on a distributor's self-business (own investments).
  • Change of Distributor Code: If an investor switches their ARN code from one distributor to another without changing the scheme/plan, a mandatory 12-month cooling-off period applies during which neither the old nor the new distributor receives trail commission (exception: voluntary cessation of business or death of the original distributor).
  • Commission Disclosure & AMC Due Diligence Mandate: Applies to large distributors meeting any 1 of 4 thresholds: (i) Presence in > 20 locations, (ii) AUM raised > Rs. 100 Crore across the industry (excluding institutional AUM), (iii) Commission received > Rs. 1 Crore p.a. across the industry, OR (iv) Commission received > Rs. 50 Lakhs p.a. from a single mutual fund.
  • Nomination for Deceased Individual MFDs: A legal heir/nominee does not need an ARN to continue receiving ongoing trail commission on existing AUM mobilized by the deceased MFD before demise. However, to transfer the clientele/AUM into the nominee's own name and service them, the nominee must hold a valid ARN + KYD and issue a 15-day notice to investors.

Chapter 7: Performance of Mutual Funds (Weightage: 12% · 6 Questions)

7.1 Return Calculation Formulas & Worked Numericals

Return Metric Exact Mathematical Formula When Used & Worked Numerical Example
1. Simple (Absolute) Return [(Ending NAV − Beginning NAV) ÷ Beginning NAV] × 100 Used for holding periods ≤ 1 year.
Ex: NAV grows from Rs. 20 to Rs. 23 in 6 months → [(23−20)/20]×100 = 15.00%.
2. Simple Annualized Return Simple Return × (365 ÷ Holding Days) or × (12 ÷ Months) Used for Money Market/Liquid/Overnight funds (<1 yr).
Ex: 1.5% return in 3 months → 1.5% × (12/3) = 6.00% p.a.
3. Compounded Annual Growth Rate (CAGR) [(Ending Value ÷ Beginning Value)(1 ÷ n) − 1] × 100 Mandatory for holding periods > 1 year.
Ex: Rs. 100 grows to Rs. 133.10 in 3 years → (1.331)1/3 − 1 = 10.00% p.a.
4. Redemption Price with Exit Load Redemption Price = Applicable NAV × (1 − Exit Load %) Ex: Applicable NAV = Rs. 50, Exit Load = 1% → 50 × (1 − 0.01) = Rs. 49.50 per unit.

7.2 Risk Drivers in Equity, Debt & Hybrid Funds

  • Equity Portfolio Risks:
    • Market / Systematic Risk: Economy-wide risk that cannot be diversified away; measured by Beta (β). Market Beta = 1.0. A fund with β > 1 is more volatile than the market; β < 1 is less volatile.
    • Company-Specific / Unsystematic Risk: Business/financial risk unique to a company/sector; reduced through portfolio diversification.
    • Liquidity Risk: Difficulty in buying or selling securities without severe price impact (highest in Small Cap & Mid Cap stocks).
  • Debt Portfolio Risks:
    • Interest Rate Risk: Bond prices and market interest rates move in opposite (inverse) directions. When interest rates rise, prices of existing fixed-income bonds fall (and vice versa). Sensitivity to interest rate changes is measured by Modified Duration / Macaulay Duration (longer maturity/duration = higher interest rate risk).
    • Reinvestment Risk: Risk that interim coupon inflows or maturing principal will be reinvested at lower prevailing interest rates when market rates fall.
    • Credit (Default) Risk & Rating Migration Risk: Risk of delay/default in principal or interest payment by issuer, or downgrade in credit rating (e.g., AAA to AA), which widens credit spreads and causes bond prices to drop.

7.3 Risk-Adjusted Returns, Mandatory Information Ratio & Benchmarks

Metric / Concept Formula Regulatory & Analytical Significance
Sharpe Ratio (Rs − Rf) ÷ Standard Deviation (σ) Measures excess return over Risk-Free Rate (Rf, 91-day T-bill) per unit of Total Risk (σ).
Treynor Ratio (Rs − Rf) ÷ Beta (β) Measures excess return per unit of Systematic Risk (β); ideal for well-diversified equity funds.
Information Ratio (IR — 2025/2026 Mandate) (Rp − Rb) ÷ Std. Dev. of Excess Return (Tracking Error) Measures consistency of active outperformance over Tier-1 Benchmark (Rb). SEBI Circular Jan 17, 2025 mandates daily disclosure of IR for all equity-oriented schemes on AMC & AMFI websites (only mandatory risk-adjusted ratio).
Tracking Error Annualized Std. Dev. of (Rp − Rb) Measures how closely a passive Index Fund/ETF replicates its index; lower tracking error is better.
TRI & Two-Tier Benchmark Total Return Index (Capital Gains + Reinvested Dividends) All MF benchmarks use TRI (w.e.f. Feb 1, 2018). Past returns of a scheme can NEVER be criteria for selecting its benchmark. Debt & Equity funds use a 2-Tier structure (Tier-1 broad category + Tier-2 strategy/style).

8.1 Pass-Through Tax Status & Capital Gains Tax Matrix (Effective July 23, 2024)

Under Section 10(23D) of the Income Tax Act, 1961, all income earned by a SEBI-registered Mutual Fund trust (dividends, interest, or capital gains) is 100% exempt from income tax at the mutual fund level. Tax is levied only in the hands of the investor upon receiving IDCW or redeeming units.

8.2 IDCW TDS, STT, Stamp Duty, Capital Loss Set-Off & Section 80C

  • Taxation of IDCW (Dividends): Dividend Distribution Tax (DDT) was abolished in February 2020. IDCW is taxed in the hands of the investor at their applicable income tax slab rate. For resident investors, the AMC deducts TDS @ 10% under Section 194K if aggregate IDCW paid exceeds Rs. 10,000 in a financial year. There is NO TDS on capital gains for resident investors (TDS applies on both IDCW and capital gains for NRIs, subject to DTAA relief).
  • Securities Transaction Tax (STT): Applicable ONLY on sale/redemption/switch-out of Equity-Oriented Mutual Fund units (STT is NIL on purchase of any mutual fund and NIL on Debt/Liquid/Overnight funds):
    • Sale/Redemption of equity-oriented units to the Mutual Fund (or delivery sale on exchange): 0.001% paid by seller.
    • Non-delivery (intraday) sale of ETF/units on stock exchange: 0.025% paid by seller.
  • Stamp Duty (Effective July 1, 2020): Levied at 0.005% on all unit issuances/purchases (lump sum, SIP, STP-in, switch-in, and IDCW reinvestment — reducing the net amount allotted by 0.005%) and 0.015% on transfer of units in demat mode. No stamp duty on redemptions.
  • Set-off & Carry Forward of Capital Losses: Capital losses cannot be set off against salary, business, or house property income.
    • Short-Term Capital Loss (STCL) can be set off against both STCG and LTCG.
    • Long-Term Capital Loss (LTCL) can be set off ONLY against LTCG.
    • Unabsorbed capital losses can be carried forward for up to 8 assessment years.
  • Section 80C (ELSS): Investment in ELSS qualifies for tax deduction up to Rs. 1,50,000 per FY for individuals and HUFs ONLY under the Old Tax Regime (not available under the New Tax Regime). Mandatory 3-year lock-in period from the date of unit allotment.

Chapter 9: Investor Services (Weightage: 20% · 10 Questions — Highest Weightage)

9.1 NFO Cycle, Direct vs. Regular Plans & Investor Eligibility

  • New Fund Offer (NFO) Timelines: An NFO (other than ELSS) can remain open for a maximum of 15 days. NFO offer price is Rs. 10 per unit. Unit allotment and dispatch of Statement of Account (SOA) must happen within 5 business days of NFO closure (2 working days for credit to Demat account); refunds for rejected applications within 5 business days (failing which the AMC pays 15% p.a. interest).
  • Direct Plan vs. Regular Plan: Direct Plan is for investors investing directly without a distributor; it has a lower TER (zero distribution commission) and consequently a separate, higher NAV than the Regular Plan of the same scheme.
  • Eligible Investors & Documentation Rules:
    • Individuals: Singly or jointly (up to a maximum of 3 joint holders). Mode of operation: Single, Joint, or Anyone or Survivor.
    • Minors (Sole Holder Rule): An investment in the name of a minor must be as a sole holder represented by a natural/court-appointed guardian (no joint holding with a minor, and no nomination permitted in a minor's folio). Payment can come from the minor's bank account or joint account with guardian. Both the Guardian's KYC and Minor's proof of date of birth/relationship are required (Sample Q4 stresses Guardian's KYC). Upon attaining majority (18 years), the folio is frozen for debits until the Minor Attaining Majority (MAM) form, PAN, and KYC of the major are updated.
    • Hindu Undivided Family (HUF): Invests through its Karta in the name of the HUF (with HUF PAN & bank account).
    • NRIs, PIOs & OCIs: Fully eligible (many AMCs restrict US/Canada residents due to FATCA compliance costs). Repatriable investments must come from NRE or FCNR accounts; Non-repatriable investments from NRO accounts.
    • Non-Individual / Institutional Investors & UBO Norms: Require PAN, KYC, MoA/AoA or Trust Deed, Board Resolution, Authorised Signatory List, and Ultimate Beneficial Owner (UBO) declaration. UBO threshold: holding > 25% of shares/capital/profits in a Company, or > 15% in a Partnership Firm, Unincorporated Association, or Trust (Listed companies and their subsidiaries are exempt).

9.3 Payment Modes, Instant Access Facility (IAF), KYC & 2026 Investor Service Reforms

  • Permitted Payment Modes & Rs. 50,000 Cash/E-Wallet Limit: Net banking, NEFT, RTGS, IMPS, UPI, NACH, One-Time Mandate (OTM), ASBA (for NFOs), AEPS, NUUP (*99# across 11 languages), and Debit Cards (Credit Cards are strictly prohibited).
    • Cash & E-Wallet Umbrella Cap: Maximum Rs. 50,000 per investor, per mutual fund, per financial year (combined limit across cash and e-wallet). Cash investments are permitted only for resident individuals, sole proprietorships, and minors without PAN/taxpayer status via physical deposit slip at designated banks, but all redemptions and IDCW payouts must be credited strictly to the investor's bank account (never paid in cash).
    • Third-Party Payments Prohibited (with 3 Exceptions): Payments must come from the first holder's bank account, except: (1) Payment by parents/grandparents/related persons on behalf of a minor up to Rs. 50,000 per transaction/SIP, (2) Employer making payroll deductions for employees or Custodian paying on behalf of FPIs, and (3) AMC transferring commission in the form of units to an MFD.
    • Multiple Bank Accounts Registration: Up to 5 bank accounts for individuals/HUFs and up to 10 bank accounts for non-individuals.
  • Instant Access Facility (IAF): Permitted ONLY in Liquid Schemes for resident individual investors via online IMPS transfer. Maximum instant redemption limit per day per scheme per investor is Rs. 50,000 OR 90% of the latest value of the investment, whichever is lower.
  • PAN & KYC Exemptions and Accessible Digital KYC (April 30, 2025 SC Ruling):
    • 4 PAN Exemptions: (1) Micro-SIPs / lump-sum investments not exceeding Rs. 50,000 per investor per MF per FY (must quote PAN Exempt KYC Reference Number — PEKRN), (2) Investors residing in the state of Sikkim, (3) Transactions on behalf of Central/State Government and court orders, and (4) UN entities/multilateral agencies exempt from tax in India.
    • Central KYC Registry (cKYCR): Operated by CERSAI since August 1, 2016 (14-digit KIN). Pursuant to the Supreme Court judgement dated April 30, 2025, SEBI mandated accessible digital KYC Norms for Persons with Disabilities (PwDs), including live video/human-assisted fallback where automated liveness checks fail.
  • Systematic Facilities & SIP Pause: SIP (Rupee Cost Averaging), SIP Top-Up, SIP Pause Facility (allows investors to temporarily pause SIP instalments for 1 to 6 months during financial emergencies with automatic resumption), SWP, STP, Switch, and DTP (IDCW Transfer Plan).
  • Nomination Reforms (2025–2026 Circulars): Under SEBI circular SEBI/HO/OIAE/OIAE_IAD-3/P/ON/2025/01650 (tested in Chapter 9 Sample Question 6) and May 2026 updates, investors can nominate up to 10 individuals in a folio (while traditional operational sections also mention up to 3 nominees; note both 10 individuals under the new 2025/2026 SEBI nomination circular and 3 nominees in legacy text). Power of Attorney (PoA) holders cannot make or change nominations. Investors can also empower a non-minor nominee to operate the account in case of the investor's physical incapacitation.
  • Transfer & Gifting of Units in SOA & Demat Mode (2026 Update): SEBI now permits off-market transfer/gifting of mutual fund units held in both Statement of Account (SOA) and Demat modes to eligible relatives (Spouses, children, parents, siblings, and parents-in-law), except for ETFs, solution-oriented retirement/children's funds, and units under lock-in (ELSS) or pledge/lien.
  • MITRA Portal, DigiLocker & Unclaimed Assets (2026 Update):
    • Qualified RTAs (CAMS & KFintech) launched MITRA (Mutual Fund Investment Tracing and Retrieval Assistant) to help investors and legal heirs trace inactive and unclaimed mutual fund folios across the industry. Holdings can also be linked with DigiLocker.
    • Unclaimed Redemption & IDCW Amounts: Deployed in a separate plan of a Liquid / Overnight scheme (AMC can charge max 0.50% p.a. management fee; zero exit load). If claimed by the investor within 3 years, paid at prevailing NAV (including appreciation); if claimed after 3 years, investor receives the value as at the end of 3 years, and appreciation beyond 3 years is transferred to the Investor Education and Protection Fund.

Chapter 10: Legal and Regulatory Environment (Weightage: 8% · 4 Questions)

10.1 Regulation 39 Investment Restrictions & SEBI Advertisement Code

  • Purpose of Investment Restrictions (Regulation 39 of SEBI MF Regulations, 2026): Primarily designed to control concentration/credit risks and protect investor interests (e.g., max 10% of NAV in a single issuer's rated debt expandable to 12% with Trustee approval; max 10% of NAV in a single company's equity for open-ended diversified schemes; no investment in unlisted/privately placed group company securities of the Sponsor).
  • SEBI Advertisement Code (Fifth Schedule):
    • Standard Warning: Every advertisement must carry the 14-word disclaimer: "Mutual Fund investments are subject to market risks, read all scheme related documents carefully" (in audio-visual advertisements, the voice-over and text must run for at least 5 seconds without background music).
    • Return Disclosure Rules:
      • Schemes in existence for > 3 years: Disclose CAGR for 1 year, 3 years, 5 years, and Since Inception, along with a point-to-point value of a standard investment of Rs. 10,000 compared against the TRI benchmark and additional benchmark.
      • Schemes in existence for < 6 months: Past performance shall NOT be advertised.
      • Schemes in existence for 6 months to 1 year: Disclose simple annualized return for the 6-month period.
      • Overnight, Liquid & Money Market Funds: Permitted to advertise simple annualized yields for 7 days, 15 days, and 30 days.
    • Celebrity Endorsements: Permitted ONLY at the industry level (e.g., "Mutual Funds Sahi Hai" by AMFI) to promote general awareness with prior SEBI approval. Celebrities are strictly prohibited from endorsing individual mutual fund schemes or AMCs.

10.2 Investors' Rights, Grievance Redressal (SCORES / SMART ODR) & AMFI Code of Ethics (ACE / AGNI)

  • Key Statutory Rights of Unitholders: Beneficial ownership of scheme assets (creditors of AMC/Sponsor cannot attach scheme assets); right to terminate the AMC or wind up a scheme by resolution of 75% of unitholders; right to inspect Trust Deed and unclaimed asset records; right to proceeds recovered from illiquid/defaulted securities within 2 years of scheme winding-up (if substantial, paid to old unitholders; otherwise transferred to Investor Education Fund).
  • Escalation Matrix for Investor Grievances:
    1. First approach the AMC / RTA Investor Service Centre (ISC).
    2. If unresolved, lodge an online complaint on SEBI's SCORES Portal (scores.sebi.gov.in).
    3. If still unsatisfied, initiate Online Conciliation and Online Arbitration via the SMART ODR Portal (smartodr.in).
  • AMFI Code of Ethics (ACE) & AGNI (AMFI Guidelines & Norms for Intermediaries):
    • Under ACE, a "Significant Unitholder" is defined as any entity holding 5% or more of the total corpus of any scheme.
    • Under AGNI, distributors must never rebate/pass back commission to investors, never split applications to earn higher incentives, and never churn portfolios unnecessarily.
    • If a breach of AGNI is alleged, the intermediary is given 3 weeks to submit a written explanation. Upon a second proven violation, AMFI cancels the distributor's ARN registration permanently and notifies all AMCs.
    • Every distributor must disclose their identity on all business cards, websites, and stationary using the exact tagline: "AMFI-registered Mutual Fund Distributor" in a clear font size of at least 12.
